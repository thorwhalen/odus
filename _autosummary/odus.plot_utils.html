odus.plot_utils¶

Plotting helpers for trajectories and matrices.

Heatmaps, life-course plots, iteration over a store’s trajectory plots, and writing a selection of trajectories out to a (multi-page) file.

stacked_trajectories puts several of them one above the other, aligned on age, year, or the years since some variable was first non-zero.

plot_trajectory is the readable rendering of a single trajectory: a gap between cells (wider every major_every years), alternating grey rows so the eye can follow a row back to its variable, optional light shading of variable groups, and per-variable colors (for instance the ones read from the source spreadsheet with row_colors_of_xls).

odus.plot_utils.plot_trajectory(df, fields=None, *, title=None, ax=None, row_colors=None, default_color='#1b2a49', row_backgrounds=('#ffffff', '#ebebeb'), row_groups=None, group_colors=None, group_tint=0.35, gap_color='white', minor_gap=0.6, major_gap=2.5, major_every=5, xtick_every=None, cell_size=0.22, fontsize=8, ylabel_right=False, x=None)[source]¶

Plot one trajectory (index: ages or years, columns: variables) readably.

Each variable is a row and each age/year a column. A cell takes its row’s color in proportion to its value, over the row’s background.

Parameters:

  • df – A trajectory DataFrame: one row per age/year, one column per variable.

  • fields – The variables (columns) to show, in order. Defaults to all.

  • title – Optional title.

  • ax – The axes to draw on. If None, a new figure is made, sized so that cells are cell_size inches and every label fits.

  • row_colors – Color of the “on” cells: a single color, a list (one per variable), or a {variable: color} mapping (missing variables get default_color). See row_colors_of_xls to reuse the colors of the source spreadsheet.

  • default_color – Color of variables that row_colors doesn’t specify.

  • row_backgrounds – Background colors, cycled row by row (the alternating greys that make it easy to follow a row). Use None for white.

  • row_groups – A {group_name: variables} mapping. Rows of a group get a light tint of its color, and a wider gap separates rows of different groups. Keep it to a few (2 to 4) groups.

  • group_colors – Colors of the groups: a list (cycled) or a {group_name: color} mapping. Defaults to DFLT_GROUP_COLORS.

  • group_tint – How strongly (0 to 1) a group color tints the background.

  • gap_color – Color of the gaps between cells.

  • minor_gap – Width (points) of the gap between two cells.

  • major_gap – Width (points) of the wider gaps: before every age/year multiple of major_every, and between row groups.

  • major_every – See major_gap. If the index isn’t integer-valued, every major_every-th column. Falsy: no wider gaps.

  • xtick_every – Label every xtick_every-th column. Defaults to every column when there’s room for it, else to major_every.

  • cell_size – Size (inches) of a cell, used when ax is None.

  • fontsize – Font size of the tick labels.

  • ylabel_right – Whether to also label the variables on the right.

  • x – Where to draw the columns on the x axis: one number per age, each one more than the previous (e.g. the years, or the years since some event). Default: column positions, labeled with the index (the ages). Plots drawn with comparable x line up (see stacked_trajectories).

Returns:

The matplotlib axes drawn on.

odus.plot_utils.row_colors_of_xls(xls_filepath, *, sheetname=None, label_col=2, data_col=3)[source]¶

Get a {variable: color} mapping from the cell colors of a spreadsheet.

Use it as the row_colors of plot_trajectory to color variables as the source spreadsheet does. A variable’s color is the fill of its label cell, or if that has none, the most common fill of its data cells. Variables with no solid RGB or indexed (palette) fill are left out, as are theme colors.

Parameters:

  • xls_filepath – Path to the .xlsx file.

  • sheetname – The sheet to read (default: the active one).

  • label_col – The (1-based) column holding the variable names.

  • data_col – The (1-based) first column holding the data.

odus.plot_utils.stacked_trajectories(df_store, fields=None, keys=None, *, x_align='age', x_label=None, k_df_to_title=<function _title_of_key>, cell_size=0.22, fontsize=8, major_every=5, xtick_every=None, **plot_kwargs)[source]¶

Plot several trajectories one above the other, aligned on a common x axis.

Parameters:

  • df_store – A mapping of trajectories (like a DfStore).

  • fields – The variables to show (default: all, per trajectory).

  • keys – The keys of df_store to plot, or a filter function of the keys.

  • x_align –

    What to align the trajectories on:

    • "age" (default) or "year" (needs df.attrs["year_of_age"], which DfStore provides);

    • a variable name: the years since that variable was first non-zero (e.g. "HEROIN": years since first heroin use). Trajectories where it never is are left out, with a warning;

    • a function x_align(df) returning the x of each age (one more from each age to the next), or None to leave the trajectory out.

  • x_label – Label of the x axis (default: from x_align).

  • k_df_to_title – k_df_to_title(key, df) gives the title of each plot.

  • cell_size – Passed on to plot_trajectory (see there).

  • fontsize – Passed on to plot_trajectory (see there).

  • major_every – Passed on to plot_trajectory (see there).

  • xtick_every – Passed on to plot_trajectory (see there).

  • plot_kwargs – Passed on to plot_trajectory (see there).

Returns:

The matplotlib figure.
